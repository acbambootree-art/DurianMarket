Ask a group chat where to go for durian tonight and two names come up more than most: Geylang and Dempsey. One is a row of brightly lit stalls along Sims Avenue and Geylang Road that never closes. The other is a single open-air stall in a carpark at Dempsey Hill, surrounded by trees and expensive restaurants.

People assume Dempsey must be the pricier one because of the postcode. That assumption is too simple. Here's what actually drives the price at each, and how to make sure you're not overpaying at either.

## The short answer

Neither area has a fixed price. Durian prices change daily with supply, and every stall sets its own signboard. You can find a good deal or a bad one in both places.

What differs is the *structure* of the market. Geylang gives you competition on one street. Dempsey gives you one stall and a nicer setting. That shapes how much bargaining power you have, which matters more than the address.

## Geylang: the durian strip

Geylang is the closest thing Singapore has to a durian district. Several long-running stalls sit within a short walk of each other along Sims Avenue and Geylang Road, and a number of them are open 24 hours. You can see them all in our [Geylang directory](/directory/geylang).

**Why it can be cheaper:** competition. When three or four stalls are within walking distance, each one knows you can walk away. You can check the signboards up and down the street, ask for a price on the same variety at two stalls, and buy from the one that gives you the better deal. That pressure tends to keep prices honest.

**The catch:** a busy strip also means a lot of first-time buyers and tourists, and not every stall treats them the same way. The usual tricks — quoting per fruit then charging per kg, weighing a different fruit, pushing the most expensive variety — are easier to pull on someone who looks unsure. Our guide to [durian stall tricks](/guides/durian-scams-singapore) covers what to watch for.

**Best for:** late-night cravings, comparing several varieties, and buyers who are happy to walk and compare.

## Dempsey: the open-air favourite

Dempsey Hill is a former British army barracks turned dining enclave, full of restaurants and bars. The durian stall here is a veteran open-air operation in the carpark, with tables under the trees. See it in our [Dempsey directory](/directory/dempsey).

**Why people expect it to be expensive:** the neighbourhood. But a carpark stall with plastic stools is not paying restaurant overheads, and the price on the signboard is set by the same daily wholesale market as everyone else's.

**Why it might cost more anyway:** there's no stall next door to walk to. Without nearby competition, you can't easily price-check on the spot, and you're less likely to walk away after driving up the hill. That isn't the same as being overcharged, but it does mean you need to do your homework before you arrive.

**What you get in return:** the setting, and range. The Dempsey stall is known for carrying a wide line-up, including less common varieties like [Black Thorn](/guides/black-thorn-vs-musang-king), [Tekka (Green Bamboo)](/guides/tekka-durian-guide) and kampung durians. If you want to try something other than Musang King, that's worth more than a small price difference.

**Best for:** a relaxed evening out, groups, and trying varieties you won't find everywhere.

## What actually decides the price you pay

The area is a small factor. These matter more:

**The variety.** The gap between a [D13](/guides/d13-durian-guide) and a premium Musang King is far larger than any gap between two neighbourhoods. Decide what you want before you compare.

**The grade.** Two stalls can both sell "Musang King" at very different prices because one is selling a higher grade. Our [Musang King grades guide](/guides/msw-grades-explained) explains what you're comparing.

**The day.** Prices move with harvests. A glut week makes everything cheaper everywhere, and a thin week pushes prices up everywhere. [Why durian prices change](/guides/why-durian-prices-change) goes into why.

**Your negotiating position.** Buying a large amount, being a regular, or simply showing you know the going rate all help. This is where Geylang's competition gives you leverage that a standalone stall can't.

## How to avoid overpaying at either

1. **Check today's price first.** Look at the [tracker](/) for the variety you want. Walk in with a number in your head.
2. **Confirm the pricing unit.** Ask whether the price is per kg or per fruit, and whether it's for whole fruit or opened flesh.
3. **Watch the weighing.** Make sure the fruit on the scale is the fruit you picked.
4. **Ask them to open it before you pay.** A good stall will replace a bad fruit. If they won't, buy elsewhere.
5. **At Dempsey, call ahead.** Ask what's in and roughly what it costs today. It saves a wasted trip up the hill.

## So which is cheaper?

If price is the only thing you care about, Geylang gives you the better odds — not because rent or location makes it automatically cheaper, but because you can compare several stalls in five minutes and walk away from a bad offer.

If you're going for the evening, the setting and the variety line-up, Dempsey is the better night out, and a well-prepared buyer won't pay much more for it.

Either way, the bigger savings come from choosing the right variety on the right week, not the right postcode. Browse the full [directory](/directory) for stalls closer to home, and check the [season calendar](/guides/durian-season-calendar) to time your visit.
