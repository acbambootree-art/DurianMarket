Walk up to any durian stall in Singapore and you'll see the same two names on the board: **Musang King** at the top, **D24** somewhere below it at roughly half the price. Both are Malaysian cultivars, both are bittersweet, and both get passed off as each other often enough that knowing the difference saves you real money.

Here's the honest comparison.

## The short answer

If you want the richest, most intense durian experience and you're willing to pay for it, get **Musang King**. If you want 80% of that experience at roughly half the price, get **D24**. Neither is "better" — they're different price points on the same flavour spectrum, and plenty of longtime durian eaters genuinely prefer D24.

## What is Musang King?

Musang King (Mao Shan Wang, 猫山王, officially registered as D197) came out of Kelantan and rose to fame through Pahang's Raub district, which still produces some of the most sought-after fruit. It's the benchmark premium durian in Singapore.

What you're paying for:

- **Flesh:** deep golden-yellow, thick, and creamy with a texture people describe as custard or cold butter
- **Taste:** intensely bittersweet — sweet up front, with a bitter, almost wine-like finish
- **Seeds:** famously small and flat, so more of the weight you pay for is edible flesh
- **The tell:** a star-shaped pattern of ridges on the base of the husk, radiating from the centre

## What is D24?

D24 (often sold as "Sultan") was *the* premium durian before Musang King took over, and it's still the best-selling mid-range variety in Singapore. Older Singaporeans grew up on it.

What you get:

- **Flesh:** pale yellow, smooth and creamy, slightly firmer than Musang King
- **Taste:** bittersweet but noticeably milder — less sugar-rush, less bitter punch, more balanced
- **Seeds:** larger than Musang King's, so a bit less flesh per kilogram
- **Character:** a subtle alcoholic aftertaste when well-ripened, which fans consider the whole point

## How to tell them apart at the stall

This matters because the most common stall trick in Singapore is selling D24 (or worse, mixed-variety flesh in a box) at Musang King prices. Look for:

1. **The star.** Turn the fruit over. Musang King has a distinct 5-pointed star pattern of brownish ridges at the base. D24 has a plain base, usually with a small ring around the stem instead.
2. **Flesh colour.** MSW is unmistakably golden, almost turmeric-toned. If the flesh is pale cream-yellow, you're looking at D24 or something else.
3. **The seed.** Open fruit on display? MSW seeds are thin and shrivelled-looking. Big round seeds mean it's not MSW.
4. **The price.** If "Musang King" is priced like D24, it's D24. Nobody discounts real MSW by 50% out of generosity.

## Which should you actually buy?

**Buy Musang King when:**

- It's peak season (supply up, prices down — see [when durian season falls](/trends))
- You're introducing someone to premium durian and want the full effect
- You're buying a small amount — the per-session cost difference is only a few dollars for one fruit

**Buy D24 when:**

- You're feeding a group and the kilos add up
- You prefer a more balanced, less overwhelming flavour — this is a legitimate preference, not a compromise
- It's off-season and MSW prices have spiked; off-season MSW is often not worth the premium anyway

## One last tip

Variety matters less than freshness and the honesty of your seller. A properly ripened D24 from a good stall beats a stale, over-refrigerated Musang King every time.
