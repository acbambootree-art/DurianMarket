The short answer: durian is cheapest in Singapore during the peak of the main Malaysian harvest, which usually lands somewhere between June and August. If you only remember one thing from this guide, remember that.

But "cheap" works on more than one clock. There is the season, there is the week inside the season, and there is the hour you walk up to the stall. Getting all three roughly right saves more than haggling ever will. And there is a version of "cheap" that isn't worth it at all.

This guide is about spending less for durian you actually enjoy. For the full month-by-month picture, see the [durian season calendar](/guides/durian-season-calendar).

## Clock one: the season

Almost all fresh durian here comes across the Causeway from Peninsular Malaysia. When the orchards there drop heavily, Singapore stalls receive more fruit than they can comfortably sell each night, and prices soften. When the orchards go quiet, the little fruit that arrives is priced for scarcity.

That gives you a rough shape to the year:

| Window (approx.) | Supply | Price pressure | Verdict for bargain hunters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak main season (often Jun – Aug) | Heavy, many varieties at once | Downward | Best time to buy, including premium varieties |
| Early main season (often May) | Building, uneven quality | Starting to fall | Wait a couple of weeks |
| Late main season (often Sep – Oct) | Tapering | Rising | Fine for casual buys, not for stocking up |
| Year-end season (Nov – Dec, if it comes) | Smaller, unreliable | Mixed | A bonus, not a plan |
| Off-season (often Mar – Apr) | Thin | Highest | Avoid if price matters |

The dates move every year with the weather, so treat the months as a guide rather than a promise. Our [durian season guide](/guides/durian-season-singapore) explains why they drift.

The practical rule: don't read the calendar, read the direction. Once supply is building, open [price trends](/trends). A rate that has fallen steadily for a week or two is telling you the peak has arrived — regardless of what month it is.

## Clock two: the week

Even inside the peak, prices move day to day. A heavy drop from the orchards can push rates down quickly, while a run of bad weather or a sudden demand spike can push them back up within days. Our guide to [why durian prices change every day](/guides/why-durian-prices-change) goes through the drivers.

Two things tend to matter:

- **Demand spikes.** Weekends, public holidays and festive periods bring more buyers out. More buyers means stalls have less reason to drop the price. If you can buy on a weekday evening instead of a Saturday night, you generally have more room.
- **Supply surges.** When a big wave of fruit arrives, sellers need to move it before it goes past its best. Those are the nights when the rate on the [live tracker](/) dips and stalls are more willing to deal.

You can't predict the surges, but you can check for them. Look at the tracker before you leave the house. If today's rate is noticeably lower than last week's, that is your day.

## Clock three: the hour

Fresh durian doesn't keep. A fruit that dropped this morning is at its best tonight or tomorrow, and a stall that bought too much has to clear it. So late in the evening, some sellers become more flexible, especially in peak season when they have plenty of stock.

This is a real effect, but it comes with a catch: late-night clearance fruit is sometimes clearance for a reason. It may be the lot that didn't sell because it was less good, or fruit that has sat for a day too long. The discount is only a win if you check what's inside.

If you go late:

- Ask to see the fruit opened before you agree on a price.
- Look for dry, intact flesh with no sour or fermented smell. The checks in [how to pick a good durian](/guides/how-to-choose-a-durian) apply doubly here.
- Plan to eat it that night. Late clearance fruit is not the lot you freeze.

Arriving early gives you the widest choice. Arriving late sometimes gives you the best rate. Pick which matters more tonight.

## Cheaper isn't only about timing

Timing is the biggest lever, but it isn't the only one. A few other choices cut the bill without cutting the enjoyment.

**Buy a cheaper variety.** The gap between the top premium varieties and the everyday ones is often larger than any seasonal swing. D24, D13 and D101 can be excellent eating. Our [D13 guide](/guides/d13-durian-guide) and [D101 guide](/guides/d101-durian-guide) explain what you get, and the [variety comparison](/guides/durian-varieties-singapore) covers the rest.

**Save premium varieties for the peak.** If Musang King or Black Thorn is your annual treat, buying it in the lean months is the most expensive way to eat durian. The [Musang King price guide](/guides/musang-king-price-guide) explains how to judge a per-kg rate.

**Buy the right amount.** Over-ordering is a hidden cost. Use the [portion guide](/guides/how-much-durian-per-person) before a group buy.

**Compare areas.** Rates differ between parts of Singapore, and the spread tends to widen when supply is tight. The [area directory](/directory) shows where sellers are clustered, so you can compare before committing.

**Stock up at the peak.** If you want durian in March, the cheapest way is often to buy it in July and freeze it. Frozen fruit loses a little texture but keeps the flavour well. See [can you freeze durian](/guides/can-you-freeze-durian).

## When cheap is a trap

Low prices attract the kind of sales tactics you should watch for.

- **A "Musang King" far below the going rate.** Premium varieties rarely get dramatically cheaper than the market, even at the peak. If the price looks too good, check the fruit. Our [guide to spotting fake Musang King](/guides/how-to-spot-fake-musang-king) shows what to look for.
- **Per-fruit prices that turn into per-kg prices.** Confirm how you are being charged before the knife goes in.
- **Weight games and swapped fruit.** Watch the scale and keep your eye on the fruit you chose. The [common durian stall tricks](/guides/durian-scams-singapore) guide covers the usual ones.

A fair price for good fruit beats a low price for fruit you throw away.

## A simple plan

1. **Wait for the main season.** Don't buy the first fruit of the year — early lots are uneven and prices haven't fallen yet.
2. **Watch the direction on [trends](/trends).** Start buying once rates have fallen for a week or two.
3. **Go on a weekday if you can.** Less competition, more room on price.
4. **Check the [live tracker](/) before every trip.** Know what fair looks like before you arrive.
5. **Open before you pay.** Especially late at night.
6. **Freeze some at the peak** if you'll want durian in the lean months.

## The short version

The cheapest durian in Singapore is peak-season durian, usually around the middle of the year, bought on a quieter day when supply is heavy. Inside that window, choosing an everyday variety over a premium one saves more than any bargaining trick. Late-night deals exist, but check the fruit before you celebrate. And whatever the calendar says, trust the numbers: [trends](/trends) for direction, the [live tracker](/) for today.
