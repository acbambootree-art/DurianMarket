If Musang King is the durian everyone name-drops, **Red Prawn** is the one regulars quietly buy every week. It's sweet rather than bitter, unmistakably orange inside, widely available in Singapore, and consistently cheaper than the top-tier fruit. For a lot of buyers it's the smartest thing on the stall.

Here's what Red Prawn actually is, who it suits, and how not to get a bad one.

## What is Red Prawn?

Red Prawn — **Ang Heh** in Hokkien, 红虾 in Mandarin, registered as **D175** — is most associated with **Balik Pulau in Penang**, though it's now grown across Malaysia and reaches Singapore from several sources. The name is about colour, not flavour: the flesh carries a distinct orange-pink cast, close to cooked prawn, that separates it from the lemon-gold of most other varieties.

It is one of the few durians you can often identify by sight alone once it's opened. That's genuinely useful in a market where labels get swapped freely.

## What it tastes like

Red Prawn sits firmly on the **sweet side** of the durian spectrum:

- **Flavour:** sweet, rich, slightly caramel-like, with only a faint bitter edge at the finish. Nothing sharp.
- **Texture:** creamy and smooth, generally wetter and looser than Musang King's dense custard. Good ones are silky; overripe ones go soupy.
- **Aroma:** present but moderate. It won't clear a room the way an intense bitter varietal does.
- **Seeds:** often larger than Musang King's, so flesh-to-seed ratio is a real thing to check before you buy.

The short version: if you found Musang King's bitterness challenging, Red Prawn is the fruit to try next. If you specifically chase bitterness, this one will taste like dessert rather than durian.

## Why it's the value pick

Three reasons Red Prawn punches above its price:

1. **Supply is healthy.** It's widely planted and travels well enough to reach Singapore in volume. Scarcity premiums don't attach to it the way they do to Black Thorn.
2. **It's forgiving.** Sweet, creamy durians are easier to enjoy across a range of ripeness than intensely bitter ones, where a slightly-off fruit tastes properly wrong.
3. **Crowds like it.** If you're feeding a table with mixed durian experience, sweet wins more people than bitter. Buying two Red Prawns instead of one Musang King often makes for a better night.

## How to pick a good one

Red Prawn's weakness is watery, over-ripe flesh. Guard against it:

- **Ask to see it opened.** Any stall selling by the fruit should open before you pay. The flesh should hold its shape when the seller lifts a seed, not slump.
- **Look for the colour.** Orange-pink is the point. If the flesh is pale yellow, you're probably not getting Red Prawn, whatever the label says.
- **Check the seed size.** Lift a piece. If it's a thin coat over a big seed, ask for another fruit — you're paying by weight and most of that weight is seed.
- **Smell for fermentation.** Alcohol or sour notes over the sweetness mean it's past its window.
- **Shake it gently.** A loud rattle usually means the flesh has shrunk away from the shell — often over-ripe or dried out.

Because Red Prawn isn't at the very top of the price ladder, it attracts less deliberate mislabeling than Musang King does. The more common problem is simply old stock being cleared, which is a seller-choice issue rather than a variety issue. ## When to buy it

Red Prawn follows the main Malaysian durian seasons, with the biggest volumes arriving through the mid-year peak and a smaller second wave later in the year. In peak weeks, supply is deep and the value case is strongest. Out of season, quality gets uneven and the price advantage over premium fruit narrows — that's the moment to either spend up or skip.

A practical habit: check the [tracker](/) before you head out, note where the Red Prawn spread sits relative to Musang King, and let the size of that gap decide what you buy that evening rather than deciding in advance.

## Who should buy Red Prawn

**Buy it if:**

- You're new to durian, or bringing someone who is
- You prefer sweet and creamy over bitter and complex
- You're buying volume for a group and want quality per dollar
- Musang King prices that week have run away from you

**Skip it if:**

- Bitterness is the reason you eat durian at all
- You want the dense, almost fudgy texture Musang King and Black Thorn deliver
- It's deep off-season and the fruit on display looks tired

## The bottom line

Red Prawn is the durian that makes regular durian-eating affordable. It won't win an argument about the best fruit in Malaysia, but it wins most arguments about the best fruit to actually buy on a Tuesday. Sweet, creamy, easy to identify, and priced where you can eat it often.
